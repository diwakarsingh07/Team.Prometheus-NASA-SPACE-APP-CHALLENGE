1. The Optical Detection Barrier
Optical telescopes rely on reflected sunlight, which falls off with the fourth power of distance (F ∝ 1/r4). At 500 AU, reflected sunlight is over 100 billion times fainter than at 1 AU, making optical discovery virtually impossible.
2. The Thermal Infrared Signature
Planet X does not shine by reflection, but by its own residual internal planetary heat. At T ≈ 40 K, Planck's radiation law dictates that its blackbody emission peaks in the mid-to-far infrared (3.0–5.0 µm), exactly within SPHEREx's spectral coverage.
3. 102 Continuous Spectral Bands
Unlike traditional single-broadband surveys, SPHEREx slices every point in the sky across 102 discrete channels (0.75–5.0 µm). This enables unambiguous extraction of methane (1.66 µm) and water-ice absorption dips to distinguish cold solar system bodies from distant red galaxies.
4. All-Sky 6-Month Astrometric Baseline
Conducting 4 full-sky surveys over 2 years provides an authentic 182.5-day baseline. This yields detectable annual parallax (π ≈ 1.2″/yr) against stationary stars, allowing precise computation of orbital inclination and semimajor axis.
