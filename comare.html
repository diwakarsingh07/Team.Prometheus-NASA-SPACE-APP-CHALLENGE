The Baby Star in an Ice Blanket (L1527)

Imagine a baby star that is only 100,000 years old (our Sun is 4.6 billion years old!). It is still wrapped inside a freezing cosmic blanket made of real water ice crystals, carbon dioxide, and dry ice. As the baby star gulps down matter from its cloud, it occasionally heats up and flickers—causing some of the surrounding space frost to melt or freeze back again. NASA observes this to understand where planet-forming water comes from!

📖 Space Words Explained (Plain English)

What is Infrared Light?

Our human eyes only see visible rainbow light. Infrared light is invisible heat radiation. Just like night-vision goggles let you see people in pitch darkness from their body heat, NASA's SPHEREx telescope uses infrared vision to peer through thick space dust clouds!

Why do we care about Frozen Water Ice in Space?

Where did Earth's oceans come from? Scientists believe billions of years ago, comets and space rocks coated in frozen water ice bombarded early Earth, filling our oceans! By mapping space frost around newborn baby stars, NASA learns how water gets delivered to new alien planets.

Why do stars move in the sky?

Everything in the Milky Way galaxy is in motion, orbiting the central supermassive black hole at hundreds of kilometers per second. While distant stars are too far away for us to notice, close stars (like Barnard's Star) visibly speed across the sky over just months!

What is an Arcsecond (")?

An arcsecond is a tiny unit used by astronomers to measure angles in the sky (1/3600th of a single degree). Seeing 1 arcsecond is like being able to spot a small coin from 4 kilometers away!

What is SPHEREx?

A NASA space telescope orbiting Earth that takes a fresh picture of the entire universe every 6 months in 102 distinct infrared colors to study cosmic inflation, water ices, and the birth of galaxies.
